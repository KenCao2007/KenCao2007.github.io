About Me

I am a third-year Ph.D. student in Statistics at the University of Wisconsin–Madison, advised by Prof. Tengyang Xie. I received my B.S. in Statistics from Renmin University of China in 2024, where I worked with Prof. Feng Zhou.

Besides research, I enjoy soccer and swimming. I also spent four wonderful years in my college Chinese orchestra.

Research

I am generally interested in sequential decision making from offline data, especially with large action spaces and long horizons. More specifically, I am interested in the following two directions, from theoretical understanding to algorithm design:

  1. Action tokenizers for continuous control [1].

  2. Stabilizing the training of off-policy and offline RL [2].

I have also worked on long-horizon reasoning in LLMs [3]. In the past, I worked on deep probabilistic models, including point processes and approximate inference.
